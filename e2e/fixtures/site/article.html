Green bonds and coastal adaptation

Coastal cities fund climate adaptation through a mix of municipal green bonds, national grants and insurance pools. Green bonds let a city borrow against future savings from avoided flood damage, and investors accept lower yields in exchange for verified environmental outcomes.

Rotterdam pioneered water squares: public plazas that double as storage basins during heavy rain. The programme was financed partly through green bonds and partly through the national Delta Fund. Jakarta, by contrast, relies on a giant sea wall financed by sovereign debt.

Municipal bonds cover less than a third of total adaptation cost in most cities studied. Insurance pools shift residual risk to the state, which raises questions about fairness for inland taxpayers who do not benefit directly from coastal protection.
